Greenhouse Crop Calendar

Which crops survive a full grow in your greenhouse, depending on the planting day. It uses Vintage Story's own temperature formula and the crop limits from the 1.22 game files: cold and heat damage, the +5 °C greenhouse bonus, and slower growth below 10 °C.

Four-greenhouse rotation

All four houses are planted on the same day. You harvest them together once the slowest crop is ripe, then every house moves one step along N → P → K → rest. The rest step lets the soil recover. Crops that ripen early stay in the ground until harvest day and are checked for damage while they wait. For each cycle the planner picks the combination with the most food per day where every crop gets a full harvest.

Temperature: yearly average, plus a seasonal swing of latitude/90 × 65 °C (coldest mid-January in the north), plus a day/night swing of 18 − 13 × rainfall °C (coldest 4:00, warmest 16:00). This is the game's own formula, without its random weather noise. The safety margin stands in for that noise.

Damage: the game checks every 3–4 hours. A check below the cold limit or above the heat limit marks the crop as damaged: the harvest drops to 50 % (75 % for carrot, parsnip, rye and fennel). After 48 hours of damage, the crop dies. Damage time slowly heals while temperatures are fine. Harvest promptly, because a ripe crop can still take frost or heat damage.

Growth: below 10 °C, each degree makes a growth step 10 % less likely. At 0 °C growth stops. "Food per block per day" is yield × satiety per item (best of raw, meal or bread) × harvest share ÷ actual days.

Opening the roof: the greenhouse bonus only applies while the room is fully closed and mostly glass on top. One missing roof block lets the room leak, so the +5 °C stops. Farmland rechecks this at most every 12 in-game hours, so the switch can lag by up to half a day. With the roof open, rain also waters the crops, which this page doesn't count.

Not modelled: soil nutrient levels, light and watering. If the world setting "harsh winters" is off, damage doesn't reduce the harvest.
