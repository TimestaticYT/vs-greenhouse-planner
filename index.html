Greenhouse Crop Calendar

Which crops survive a full grow in your greenhouse, depending on the planting day. It uses Vintage Story's own temperature formula and the crop limits from the 1.22 game files: cold and heat damage, the +5 °C greenhouse bonus, and slower growth below 10 °C.

Measure the weather offset

The game adds random weather noise to the formula: a slow part (usually within ±4 °C) that stays for many in-game months, and a fast part (about ±1 °C) that changes within hours. Run /wgen pos climate and type its first value (Temperature), or the temperature your game shows you outside, and the in-game hour. Enter the in-game date and hour of the reading. Take two or three readings at different times and use the average. Measure again about once per in-game year. With an offset set, a safety margin of about 1.5 °C is enough.

No admin rights? Set up the climate from temperature readings

The /wgen commands need admin rights. Instead, note the temperature your game shows you a few times, outside and not inside the greenhouse, with the in-game date and hour. (1) On one day, take a reading around 4:00 (coldest) and one around 16:00 (warmest): that gives the day/night swing, which sets the rainfall. (2) In another season, take two more readings; mid-winter and mid-summer work best. That separates the yearly average from the latitude. The planner fits the game's temperature formula to your readings. With readings from only one season it keeps the latitude you entered and works out the yearly average and rainfall, so that average is only right if the latitude is about right. The weather noise ends up in the yearly average, so the weather offset goes back to 0.

Your crops and fields

Untick the crops you have no seeds for. The rotation, the single-field plan and "Plant now" only plan with ticked crops. The calendar and "All crops at a glance" still show every crop, so you can see what you are missing.

The default of 684 is four houses of 171 farmland blocks: a 14 × 14 house (196 blocks, the largest room the game allows) minus 25 water blocks, with water every third block (rows and columns 1, 4, 7, 10 and 13), which keeps all farmland at 75 % moisture. Enter your own count if you lay out water differently.

Type how many seeds you have next to each crop. The rotation then plants only as many blocks as you have seeds, fills spare blocks with another crop of the same nutrient that is ripe in time, and counts the seeds you get back. Update the seed counts whenever your real stock differs, for example after a frost or a bad harvest.

All crops at a glance

Food per block per day for one grow on fully recovered soil, in warm weather (10 °C or more, no damage), with your growth speed and days per month. Each item counts in its best form: a cooking-pot meal, or perfectly baked bread for grains and cassava (part-baked is half, charred 70 %). Each column is one soil fertility; yours is highlighted and sets the order.

Four-greenhouse rotation

All four houses are planted on the same day. You harvest them together once the slowest crop is ripe, then every house moves one step along N → P → K → rest. The rest step lets the soil recover. Crops that ripen early stay in the ground until harvest day and are checked for damage while they wait. For each cycle the planner picks the combination with the most food per day. With "Also damaged harvests", a crop that takes frost or heat damage but survives is planted too, because 50–75 % of a harvest (and the seeds back) beats an empty house; its share shows as an orange chip. Flax can take the K house: its grain makes bread and its fibers come on top. "Flax in the K house" grows that share of the K house as flax instead of food, for linen and windmill sails, up to the whole house; the rest house always rests. The Hunger tab recommends how much flax your group can afford while keeping spare food. The number next to the nutrient is that house's level at planting.

Tries which months to keep the greenhouse closed (+5 °C) and which to open the roof, for this rotation and your crops. Only houses set to "Greenhouse, roof months" follow it; the others stay as you set them above.

Food needs of your group

How much satiety your players eat in one in-game year, from the game's hunger code. Just being online costs 691 satiety per in-game day; walking or swimming adds 3 times that for the time spent moving, sprinting 9 times. The game scales hunger with the speed of time, so per in-game day it stays the same. Blackguards (trait "ravenous") get hungry 30 % faster. Players in creative mode don't get hungry.

One row per player, or one row for several players who play alike (set "Players" above 1). Each row has its own class, share of game time online and activity level.

Food from outside the fields

Meat, fish, eggs, cheese, fruit trees, berries, foraging and trading. "Of all food" lowers how much your fields have to grow. The food-group fields say how much of each group's need comes from elsewhere, for example 100 % protein if your group eats enough meat; the fields then only have to cover the rest of that group. Dairy always comes from animals.

Food groups and health

The game tracks five food groups. Each full group raises max health by 2.5, so all five give +12.5. Eating adds satiety ÷ 2.5 to that food's group, and full groups slowly drain. Keeping a group full only takes about 9 % of what a player eats from that group (dairy about 5 %), so the rest can come from whatever grows best. The bars compare your harvest per group with what keeps it full for the whole group.

Plant now

One field, replanted all year

For a single field outside the four-house rotation that you replant the day it is ripe. Starting on the "Plant now" date, each row is the next crop: the one with the most food per block per day (with "Also damaged harvests" on the rotation, damaged but surviving crops count with their reduced harvest). "Soil" is that crop's nutrient level at planting, after what the crops before it used up, and the growth speed it gives.

The same field on the calendar

uses Nuses Puses K

Temperature: yearly average, plus a seasonal swing of latitude/90 × 65 °C (coldest mid-January in the north), plus a day/night swing of 18 − 13 × rainfall °C (coldest 4:00, warmest 16:00). This is the game's own formula, without its random weather noise. The safety margin stands in for that noise.

Damage: the game checks every 3–4 hours. A check below the cold limit or above the heat limit marks the crop as damaged: the harvest drops to 50 % (75 % for carrot, parsnip, rye and fennel). After 48 hours of damage, the crop dies. Damage time slowly heals while temperatures are fine. Harvest promptly, because a ripe crop can still take frost or heat damage.

Growth: below 10 °C, each degree makes a growth step 10 % less likely. At 0 °C growth stops. "Food per block per day" is yield × satiety per item (best of raw, meal or bread) × harvest share ÷ actual days.

Soil: each growth step uses up part of the crop's nutrient (N, P or K), and the next step's length depends on what is left: above 75 the crop grows 10 % faster, 51–75 normal speed, 36–50 at 90 %, 21–35 at 60 %, 6–20 at 30 %, 5 or less at 10 %. Medium soil starts at exactly 50, so it is already in the 90 % band. Unripe crops and empty soil recover nutrients up to the soil's fertility, about 1.7 per day (the crop's own nutrient a third as fast). A ripe crop stops all recovery. Fertility doesn't change the harvest amount, only the speed. The calendar grid and "Plant now" assume freshly recovered soil. The rotation and the shelf plan carry each soil's levels from one crop to the next. Fertilizer is not counted.

Opening the roof: the greenhouse bonus only applies while the room is fully closed and mostly glass on top. One missing roof block lets the room leak, so the +5 °C stops. Farmland rechecks this at most every 12 in-game hours, so the switch can lag by up to half a day. With the roof open, rain also waters the crops, which this page doesn't count.

Not modelled: fertilizer, light and watering. If the world setting "harsh winters" is off, damage doesn't reduce the harvest.
